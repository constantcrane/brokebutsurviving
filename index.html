The familiar scene

Where did all my money go?

It is usually not a dramatic crash. It is a Tuesday.

01Salary hits your account.
02Rent gets paid.
03Food gets ordered.
04Subscriptions renew.
05A few “just this once” purchases happen.
And suddenly you are checking your balance thinking:
How am I broke already?

Being single is a cost structure, not a character flaw. Every fixed cost sits on one paycheck. The book calls that the bachelor tax.

You cannot shame that gap out of existence. You can choose which parts of it you still pay.

“A boring, fully paid month beats an impressive lifestyle you cannot renew.”

Four weeks. One owner layer each week. Measurable, not motivational.

Map the last 30 days, open or nickname your Floor account, transfer something, and stock fallback food.

WEEK 02

WEEK 03

Build the plate

Choose three dinner patterns, batch once, freeze portions, pack lunches, and shop from a list.

WEEK 04

Make it stick

Cap three Rest categories, update the debt board, and raise the automatic Floor transfer by an amount you will not notice in a week.

Questions, answered

Before you click buy.

Is this only for bachelors?

The title is for the reader who asked for that frame, but the math is for anyone managing one income and one-person food waste.

Do I need to be good with money?

No. The book starts with the opposite assumption: you need visibility, a workable system, and a few repeatable moves.

Is this an investment book?

No. It focuses on everyday money management: housing, food, cashflow, debt, savings and practical income levers.

Is this extreme budgeting?

No. The goal is not to become a monk. It is to stop bleeding money and build a boring, fully paid month.

Can I use it if my income is irregular?

The book includes a method for mapping the last 90 days and budgeting the next month on the lowest of those months.

Start with the numbers

Being broke does not have to be your identity.

Broke is a moment. Surviving is a practice. Start knowing your numbers, making deliberate choices, and building systems that make saving and spending easier.
